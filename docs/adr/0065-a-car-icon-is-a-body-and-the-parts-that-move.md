# ADR-0065: A car icon is a body and the parts that move

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: Accepted — built 2026-09-04
Repos: rdm7-desktop (`src/tauri-overlay.html`, `tools/check_carown.js`)
Relates to: ADR-0011 (nothing is drawn that was not measured), ADR-0062 (the
picture is the simulator)

## Context

Studio draws the playhead as a car. The part of that which is *not* decoration
is very good and stays exactly as it is:

- the front wheels turn to `(how hard the car is turning) − (how sideways it
  is)`, weighted by the confidence the angle engine puts on the second term, so
  a gripping car winds lock in and a sliding one hands you opposite lock and
  neither is drawn on
- the tail lights come from a brake channel where there is one and from
  deceleration where there is not, sharing `GP_BRAKE_G` with the corner table
  so the map and the table cannot disagree about where he braked
- hovering a car in the picker drives it through a corner using **the same two
  functions the map uses**, so a car that demonstrates in the picker is a car
  that works on the map

Two things around it were not good.

### The choice is finer than the medium

`GP_CAR_NOMINAL_PX` is 22 and the map clamps a car to 30–88 px, so at the
bottom of the range **one glyph unit is 1.36 screen pixels**. Measured against
the shipped artwork:

| Feature | Units | Pixels at 30 px |
|---|---|---|
| Door mirror | 1.95 × 1.55 | 2.7 × 2.1 |
| A Skyline tail lamp | ⌀1.16 | ⌀1.6 |
| Roof inset from the glass | 0.7 | 1.0 |
| Silvia kamm tail vs AE86 square tail | ~0.3 | 0.4 |

Six of the seven cars differ only in features between a half and three pixels
across. On the map they are the same blob. The picker offers a choice the
medium cannot show, which is the definition of a gimmick: detail that exists to
be listed rather than to be seen. It is also, at present, the *only* thing the
picker communicates — nothing on that screen said how big a car icon actually
is.

Four of the seven are also named after real, trademarked cars. That is a
product and legal call rather than a design one and nothing here changes it —
it is recorded because it was noticed.

### A custom car lost everything that carried information

`gpCarGlyph` returned, for a user's own PNG, a bare `<image>` and nothing else.
So `gpFrontWheels` found no `.wheel`, `gpBrakeLamps` found no `.brake`, and
`gpCarDemo` bailed out with a comment saying exactly that — *"a PNG of your own
has no parts to move"*. Drawing your own car traded every part of the marker
that reacts to the recording for the one part that does not, silently, and the
picker could not have shown you because the custom swatch was an `<img>` rather
than the glyph.

## Decision

**A car icon is a BODY and the PARTS THAT MOVE.** The body is a picture — ours
or yours. The parts are drawn by the app on top of it, at a geometry that
travels with the picture.

`gpCarGlyphFor("custom", len)` emits **wheels, then the body image, then the
lamps**. Under and over, in that order, and for a reason each way: only the part
of a wheel that protrudes past the bodywork should show on a solid silhouette —
exactly as it does on the built-ins, where the wheel rects run wider than the
shell — and it is what lets the template's empty wheel wells show a whole wheel
through them. The lamps have to be on top or a solid body hides the one part
that lights up.

Nothing in the steering or braking engines changed. The class vocabulary —
`shell`, `wheel`, `glass`, `roof`, `mirror`, `panel`, `trim`, `lamp`, `brake` —
was already the contract; the custom path simply never honoured it.

### Geometry as fractions of the icon's square

`GP_CAROWN_DEF` is read off the built-ins: front axle at −0.286 of the square,
rear at +0.259, track 0.509, tyre 0.084 × 0.150, lamps at 0.436. Fractions
rather than units or pixels because the same numbers serve two things drawn at
wildly different sizes — the glyph (a 22-unit box on the map) and the template
(a 1024 px canvas you paint in). One set and they cannot disagree; two sets and
the wheels land in the wrong place on somebody's car and nobody can see why.

Every value out of the settings file is clamped: a track of 40 puts the wheels
off the *map*, not off the car, and no control on screen could bring them back.

**The rear track is 2.8% wider than the front**, as a ratio rather than a fifth
slider. Every one of the seven built-ins does this — from +0.9% on the police
car to +8.5% on the AE86, whose comment says so out loud. It is under a pixel
and a half on the map and it is the difference between a car and a shopping
trolley.

### The template

Generated by the app from those same fractions, so it can never describe a car
Studio would draw differently — and it follows the sliders, so moving the axles
for a long-wheelbase ute and downloading it again puts the wells where that
car's wheels are.

- 1024 × 1024, **transparent**. A solid background exports as a square tile on
  the map, which the importer already warns about and which would have been the
  template's own fault.
- The car's box is drawn at 76% of the canvas rather than the 92% the importer
  scales to. The importer trims to ink and rescales, so the frame's size on the
  page is free — only the *ratio* between the frame and the wheel positions has
  to hold. Spending the difference on margins is what keeps the words off the
  car.
- The wells are drawn as a hole to **leave empty**, not a shape to fill: a wheel
  painted into the picture would sit under a wheel that turns.
- Every guide is one blue, `GP_CARTPL_RGB`, so the importer can ask "are these
  still here?" — the commonest way to get a template wrong is to paint the car
  and forget the guide layer. It does not break the geometry (the dashed frame
  *is* the bounds the trim wants), so it is worth saying rather than refusing.
  Measured against the whole canvas, not against the car: the guide layer is a
  fixed amount of ink while the car is however much of it somebody drew.

### The editor says how big it really is

The Car icon group now shows the chosen car at 184 px, and beside it **at 30 px
and 88 px on a green ground** — the two sizes the map clamps to. Judging a car
icon at picker size is judging it somewhere nobody ever sees it, and that is how
a custom car came to lose its wheels without anyone noticing.

The custom swatch is drawn through `gpCarGlyphFor` like every other swatch, so
it carries its wheels and its lamps and demonstrates on hover.

## Consequences

- Sliders are offered only when a custom car is *chosen*, not merely stored:
  against a built-in, whose wheels are drawn into its own artwork, they would
  do nothing.
- Tyre width is quoted as a multiplier, not in millimetres. The glyph's tyre is
  deliberately fatter than a real one — 8.4% of the car's length where a
  245-section on a 4.6 m car is 5.3% — because at 30 px a true-width tyre is
  under half a pixel. "386 mm" would have been arithmetic dressed up as a
  measurement of somebody's car.
- The live car cannot take the red `shell` fill when its body is a photograph,
  so it takes a red halo instead.
- The picker still offers seven near-identical silhouettes. This ADR does not
  reduce that set; it makes the size honest so the next person to look at it can
  see the problem.

## Checks

`tools/check_carown.js` — 35 assertions. The ones worth having: the app-drawn
wheels land on the Skyline's own axles and track to a tenth of a unit (read out
of `GP_CARS` itself, not a copy of its numbers); `gpFrontWheels` picks the front
*pair* out of them and rotates about the wheel centres; a settings file saying
`track: 40` is clamped rather than believed; the artwork key moves when an axle
moves but a built-in ignores the sliders entirely; the wheels are emitted before
the body and the lamps after it; and the template's wheel wells sit at the same
fraction of the car's own length as the glyph's wheels do, which is the one
number that has to hold across both.
